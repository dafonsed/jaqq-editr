# Reference comparison

Three projects are now indexed against their available original media. Matching finished exports were found locally and sampled at multiple timeline positions.

| Reference | Finished export | Typical gameplay selection (median) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rainbow Six Siege | 2:56 | 1.69 seconds |
| Fortnite | 4:04 | 2.06 seconds |
| MW4 | 3:03 | 1.90 seconds |

The selection figures describe nominal base-track source runs, including source clips inside compound clips and merging adjacent entries that continue through consecutive source frames. They do not count caption changes as new gameplay selections. Upper-track coverage, speed changes and transitions still need rendered verification, so these are structural reference measurements rather than a count of every visible shot.

The earlier 2.27-second Rainbow Six figure described only direct selections from its main long recording. The broader 1.69-second figure also includes other gameplay sources and nominal nested selections; it is not a correction to the narrower measurement.

## What repeats

- Short, specific action selections recur across all three projects. The useful action is often only part of a much longer encounter.
- Shot lengths vary. Keep enough of an explanation or action to understand it; do not impose the median as a cutting interval.
- Purposeful menu demonstrations appear in the references. A blanket rule to remove all menu footage would remove useful material.
- Captions, zooms, framing and effects provide emphasis in the sampled finished exports. These are a separate layer from choosing the footage.
- Retained source footage can have no transcribed microphone words. Word-only selection is insufficient.
- Total duration follows the material. No three-minute goal, fixed compression ratio or required amount of removed footage is set.

## A project/export difference that matters

The Fortnite DRP extends to 5:43.70, but its matched finished export ends at 4:04.27. A separate recording sits at project time 5:25.22-5:43.70, after a long gap. That unexported clip is excluded from the reference library. It must not become an example of a long lobby shot to keep.

## What is ready

The library contains the three source-selection maps, compound ancestry, media availability checks, matched export limits, and sampled source/export contact sheets. The known eight-cut Rainbow Six section still matches the earlier live Resolve validation. Original projects and recordings were not modified.

This is a reference library and an editing specification, not a claim that a standalone model has learned to produce finished-quality edits. Exact sound placement and audible joins have not been evaluated by listening end to end.

The next selector should be judged by whether it preserves worthwhile action and commentary while removing the intervening waits, against these actual reference choices. Matching a shot-length average alone would not demonstrate that it works.

## Commentary comparison completed

The main source recordings used in both new references have now been transcribed from their explicitly named Microphone tracks. The reference library has 220 source selections with cached word timing across the three projects. This includes nominal compound placements and desktop demonstration footage as well as gameplay.

A simple baseline that keeps recognised words with a quarter-second margin recovers approximately 44% (Rainbow Six), 54% (Fortnite), and 37% (MW4) of the unique source-time coverage kept in the available reference mappings. These are partial structural source-coverage measurements, not viewer retention scores or an evaluation of the audible final mix. They demonstrate why selecting solely from speech would lose a large amount of deliberately retained picture.

All three reference maps pass timeline bounds and source-range consistency checks. The later unexported Fortnite clip is excluded, and the original eight-cut Rainbow Six calibration remains exact. The current automatic-edit specification is saved in analysis/editing-style/reference-guided-policy.json, with no target runtime.
